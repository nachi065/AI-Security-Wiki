    CERT-In AI Security Auditing Guidelines Crosswalk

    Purpose: Let a team preparing for an AI security audit in India find the wiki controls and test cases that answer each of the nine CERT-In audit domains, and see which auditor checks the wiki cannot help with.

    Audience: Organisations in India that build, buy or run AI systems, CERT-In empanelled auditing organisations, internal audit, GRC teams, security architects.

    Using the two together: Use the Guidelines to set the audit scope, the risk tier and the auditor's checklist, because that is what an Indian auditor works from. Use this wiki for the control design and the test cases that produce the evidence those checklists ask for.

    About the guidelines and this page

    Artificial Intelligence (AI) Security Auditing Guidelines, subtitled Framework for Secure, Ethical and Auditable Artificial Intelligence Systems, were prepared by Sub-group 03 of the CERT-In Working Group on Indian Standards for Cybersecurity Auditing. Version 1.0 is dated 4 November 2026 and runs to 107 pages.

    They cover AI systems developed, procured, deployed or operated by organisations in India, and AI systems that process Indian data or serve Indian users. They are addressed to government bodies, public sector entities, critical infrastructure operators, regulated entities, private organisations, AI developers and service providers, internal and external auditors, and CERT-In empanelled auditing organisations.

    What the framework contains:

    • Four risk tiers, Low to Critical, scored from eight inherent-risk factors. The tier sets how deep the audit goes, how often it runs, how independent the auditor must be, and how much documentation and monitoring is expected.
    • Nine core audit domains. Each has an objective, an alignment to the seven guiding principles and to ISO 42001 and NIST AI RMF, a table of audit controls, an auditor checklist and tier-by-tier applicability.
    • Advanced AI security requirements for generative AI, RAG, agentic AI, multimodal AI, multi-agent systems and autonomous or cyber-physical AI, plus twelve capabilities that escalate the tier.
    • A six-phase audit lifecycle, with auditor competence and independence requirements and rules for AI-assisted audit work.
    • Four finding severities with remediation timelines, an assurance opinion scale, and a five-level maturity model indexed to the nine domains.
    • Seven guiding principles aligned with MeitY's India AI Governance Guidelines, which section 15 maps sutra by sutra and pillar by pillar.

    The Guidelines reference ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894, ISO/IEC 27001, NIST AI RMF, MITRE ATLAS and the OWASP machine learning and LLM lists. Each of those has its own crosswalk in this section, so a mapping made here can be followed into the control detail through those pages.

    Unlike the generated crosswalks, this page is maintained by hand. The Guidelines number their requirements as document sections, so there is no identifier for a control record to cite.

    Verify before use. The domain names, section numbers, tier bands, finding severities and dates on this page were read from a copy of version 1.0 that the wiki maintainers received on 4 November 2026 [Verified against that copy]. No CERT-In web address for that version was recorded, so check that your copy matches the one CERT-In publishes. The Guidelines are guidance in themselves; they bind an organisation when a sector regulator, a contract or an empanelment requirement adopts them. The mapping below is this wiki's own judgement and has not been reviewed by CERT-In.

    Match is Direct when the wiki has a control and at least one test case for the requirement, Partial when it covers part of it, as the notes explain, and Gap when the wiki does not cover it.

    Nine core audit domains

    § Audit domain Main wiki controls Test cases Match What the wiki covers, and what it leaves out
    8.1 AI Governance and Accountability AI-CTRL-012, AI-CTRL-011, AI-CTRL-037, AI-CTRL-042, AI-CTRL-038 TC-L02-001, TC-L02-004, TC-L02-005, TC-L01-001, TC-L03-010, TC-L03-044 Direct Board-approved policy with a review cycle, the governance committee's decision record, a RACI that is enforced, the use-case register with risk tier and owner, impact assessment before deployment and on material change, and periodic control attestation. The external-requirements register the domain asks for is closest to AI-CTRL-013 and TC-L02-020.
    8.2 Data Lifecycle, Quality and Privacy AI-CTRL-017, AI-CTRL-029, AI-CTRL-013, AI-CTRL-018, AI-CTRL-019 TC-L13-002, TC-L10-008, TC-L10-031, TC-L10-033, TC-L13-003, TC-L03-015, TC-L10-023 Direct Authorised purpose and source, classification and least-privilege access, lineage from source to output, poisoning and label-flipping detection, retention and erasure, and the boundary between development and production data. Data quality is covered as a profile per dataset under AI-CTRL-029, with measured thresholds in the ISO/IEC 5259-2 characteristics, which is what the domain asks for.
    8.3 Model Risk Management AI-CTRL-030, AI-CTRL-031, AI-CTRL-033, AI-CTRL-028, AI-CTRL-011 TC-L12-001, TC-L14-004, TC-L12-019, TC-L14-002, TC-L12-024, TC-L14-013 Partial The registry, immutable version history, model cards and AI-BOM, release gates, retirement with weight disposal, and rollback are all covered. What is missing is validation by a function independent of the model developers, covering conceptual soundness and stress and edge-case testing, which the Guidelines require from Tier 2 upwards and make mandatory for Tier 3 and Tier 4. The wiki records the exposure as AI-R39 and maps it to AI-CTRL-033, AI-CTRL-034 and AI-CTRL-038, but no control text requires the validator to be independent of the build team.
    8.4 AI Ethics, Bias and Fairness AI-CTRL-041, AI-CTRL-042, AI-CTRL-012 TC-L03-031, TC-L03-032, TC-L03-043, TC-L12-033 Partial Bias testing before release and on material change with recorded method and thresholds, adverse-impact analysis for consequential decisions, a versioned bias profile with the application boundaries it was validated within, and fairness shift monitoring in production. Two of the domain's audit controls have no counterpart: screening the feature set for proxy variables that stand in for a protected attribute, and counterfactual testing that changes a protected attribute and checks the output does not move. Both appear in the Tier 3 and Tier 4 rows.
    8.5 Explainability and Transparency AI-CTRL-041, AI-CTRL-008, AI-CTRL-039, AI-CTRL-045 TC-L03-029, TC-D12-006, TC-L08-040, TC-L05-021, TC-L03-041 Partial Explanation records for automated decisions, logs detailed enough to reconstruct what the model saw, grounded output with verified citations, model cards for auditors, and the inputs and confidence a reviewer is given. The domain also asks for the choice of explainability technique to be documented with its limitations, and for explanations to be tested for fidelity, stability, consistency and reproducibility. Neither is a wiki control.
    8.6 Security, Adversarial Resilience and Incident Reporting AI-CTRL-005, AI-CTRL-034, AI-CTRL-028, AI-CTRL-032, AI-CTRL-031, AI-CTRL-035, AI-CTRL-043 TC-L01-005, TC-D08-002, TC-L12-010, TC-L12-005, TC-L12-013, TC-D12-004, TC-L03-019, TC-L17-004 Direct Threat modelling across data, models, prompts, RAG, tools and infrastructure; adversarial robustness testing; VAPT of model endpoints, APIs and pipelines; artefact integrity and signature verification; supply-chain scanning; infrastructure hardening; and incident response with preserved state and tamper-evident logs. This domain is the wiki's centre of gravity. The six-hour reporting window and the 180-day log retention the Guidelines cite are left as parameters for the assessor to fill in; row IN10 of the India hub carries the figures from the CERT-In Directions.
    8.7 Continuous Monitoring, Drift Management and Validation AI-CTRL-034, AI-CTRL-009, AI-CTRL-041, AI-CTRL-031, AI-CTRL-040 TC-L12-033, TC-L14-016, TC-L12-018, TC-L16-013, TC-L17-028 Direct Input drift, prediction drift, accuracy decay and fairness shift against a validation baseline, with a named owner and a defined response; monitoring coverage and drift signals after deployment; behaviour drift when a provider changes a pinned model; and re-validation before go-live, after any change and at least quarterly under AI-CTRL-034. Retraining governance is the thinner part: the wiki gates the release but has no control for data freshness requirements or an approval workflow specific to a retraining cycle. Predictive Model Monitoring and Adversarial ML is the standard to hand an auditor for this domain.
    8.8 Human Oversight and Decision Accountability AI-CTRL-045, AI-CTRL-023, AI-CTRL-022, AI-CTRL-025, AI-CTRL-044, AI-CTRL-008 TC-L01-012, TC-L01-022, TC-L06-015, TC-L06-016, TC-L01-021, TC-L03-040, TC-L09-025 Direct The oversight model recorded and tested by tier, human-over-the-loop authorisation when per-action approval is dropped, approval gates that resist bypass and fatigue, a tested kill switch and safe state, reviewer competence, and an audit trail that names the approver. AI-CTRL-045 answers the effectiveness-monitoring requirement directly: it measures override rates, review time and seeded-error detection to catch oversight that has become a formality.
    8.9 Third-Party and AI Supply-Chain Assurance AI-CTRL-014, AI-CTRL-031, AI-CTRL-024, AI-CTRL-040, AI-CTRL-007 TC-L16-008, TC-L16-009, TC-L16-003, TC-L16-001, TC-L16-012, TC-L16-020, TC-L10-025, TC-L16-025 Direct Due diligence and assurance evidence, provenance and integrity of hub-sourced models, the AI-BOM and SBOM, contractual security, notification and audit rights, provider deprecation and behaviour-change monitoring, consumer-side logging of AI-as-a-service, dependency scanning, and exit, escrow and fallback arrangements. The Vendor Evaluation section holds the scoring instrument.

    Of the nine domains, 6 are a direct match and 3 are partial. None is unmapped.

    Risk tiering

    The Guidelines score eight inherent-risk factors from 1 to 4, before any credit for control effectiveness, and read the total against four bands: 8 to 12 is Tier 1, 13 to 19 Tier 2, 20 to 26 Tier 3 and 27 to 32 Tier 4. Seven characteristics escalate the tier whatever the score says, among them autonomous safety-critical action, privileged production access, irreversible harm and a system that cannot be reliably interrupted or rolled back.

    The wiki's T01 intake and tiering form also scores eight questions, from 0 to 3, into three bands. The factors line up well enough to run one form for both, once the scale and the bands are changed.

    CERT-In risk factor Closest T01 question
    F1 Impact severity 1. Effect on a person's legal rights, finances, employment, health or access to services
    F2 Autonomy and actionability 2. Degree of automation, with 7. Autonomy and tool access
    F3 Data sensitivity 4. Sensitivity of data
    F4 Scale and exposure 3. Scale in users or decisions per month
    F5 Reversibility and recoverability 5. Reversibility of harm
    F6 Operational or sector criticality 6. Regulatory exposure
    F7 Adversarial exposure No T01 question. Add one for internet-facing use and for a known hostile threat environment.
    F8 Complexity and auditability 8. Vendor opacity and third-party dependence, in part. T01 does not ask about model opacity or emergent behaviour.

    To use the wiki's form for a CERT-In audit, add the two missing factors, rescore each factor from 1 to 4 and read the total against the four bands. The tier then sets the audit mechanism: self-assessment at Tier 1, documented internal audit at Tier 2, independent third-party audit by a CERT-In empanelled organisation at Tier 3, and the same with continuous monitoring at Tier 4. Small firms get proportionate documentation for Tier 1 and Tier 2 systems, and the Guidelines state plainly that Tier 3 and Tier 4 obligations do not shrink because the organisation is small.

    Advanced AI security requirements

    Section 9 adds requirements for particular architectures on top of the nine domains. The wiki is at its deepest here, since the control library and the test cases were written around these patterns.

    § Requirement set Main wiki controls Test cases Match
    9.3 Generative AI: trust boundaries between system, user, retrieved and tool content; output integrity and filtering; hallucination management; fine-tuning data governance; deepfake and synthetic media policy; copyright; disclosure that the user is dealing with AI AI-CTRL-002, AI-CTRL-005, AI-CTRL-020, AI-CTRL-039, AI-CTRL-029, AI-CTRL-046 TC-L07-001, TC-L07-035, TC-L05-018, TC-L12-017, TC-L03-035, TC-L03-028 Direct. The Guidelines say input sanitisation alone may not be relied on against prompt injection, which is the position AI-CTRL-005 and the L07 cases take.
    9.6 RAG and vector databases: knowledge-source provenance, secure ingestion, document and chunk-level access control, tenant isolation, indirect injection, poisoned sources, stale content, deletion propagation, retrieval logging AI-CTRL-018, AI-CTRL-019, AI-CTRL-032 TC-L11-004, TC-L11-007, TC-L11-008, TC-L11-010, TC-L11-018, TC-L11-024, TC-L11-003 Direct
    9.7 Agentic AI: documented agent identity, authorised objectives, approved tools, permission boundaries and action limits, no inherited permissions, approval gates, execution logging, emergency interruption and credential revocation, an approved inventory of tool and MCP connections AI-CTRL-006, AI-CTRL-015, AI-CTRL-016, AI-CTRL-022, AI-CTRL-024, AI-CTRL-025, AI-CTRL-027 TC-D09-001, TC-L06-013, TC-L06-017, TC-L06-024, TC-L06-006, TC-L06-008, TC-L06-028, TC-L06-022, TC-L06-023 Direct. The requirement that dynamically discovered tools pass a provenance and integrity check before production use is tested by TC-L06-008 and TC-L06-047.
    9.8 Multimodal AI: hidden instructions in media, cross-modal injection, manipulated or spoofed files, modality-specific adversarial examples, inconsistent behaviour across modalities AI-CTRL-021, AI-CTRL-003, AI-CTRL-005 TC-D11-002, TC-D11-004, TC-D11-008, TC-D11-010, TC-D11-012, TC-D11-013 Direct
    9.9 Multi-agent AI: unique agent identity, inter-agent authentication, delegation boundaries, privilege containment, agent messages treated as untrusted, context and memory isolation, limits on recursive delegation, cascading failure controls, action traceability, emergency containment AI-CTRL-015, AI-CTRL-006, AI-CTRL-026, AI-CTRL-032, AI-CTRL-025 TC-L06-038, TC-L06-040, TC-L06-042, TC-L06-044, TC-L06-045, TC-L06-020, TC-L06-021, TC-L06-046, TC-L07-029 Direct. The nine-point auditor check in this section maps almost one to one onto the L06 agent-to-agent cases.
    9.10 Autonomous and cyber-physical AI: operating boundaries, sensor and input integrity, safety interlocks independent of the model, safe state and degraded mode, manual takeover, enforced physical action limits, simulation and fault injection, secure updates, physical event traceability, emergency shutdown AI-CTRL-040, AI-CTRL-025, AI-CTRL-022 TC-L05-013, TC-L01-021, TC-L06-024, TC-L01-018 Partial. Fail-closed behaviour, a tested kill switch, blast-radius caps and fallback plans carry the software half. Sensor spoofing, interlocks that run independently of the model, hardware-in-the-loop and fault-injection testing, and actuator or speed limits enforced in hardware are outside the wiki's scope, as its limitations page says.

    Section 9.5 lists twelve capabilities that escalate the tier, among them persistent memory that shapes later actions, autonomous multi-step planning, delegation to other agents or dynamically chosen tools, privileged production access and opaque third-party dependencies. They make a good checklist against an agent design and sit close to the questions in the use-case risk triage.

    The rest of the framework

    § What it covers Where the wiki meets it
    7 Six-phase audit lifecycle, auditor competence and independence, pre-audit documentation request, scope limitations and alternative assurance The Audit and Evidence Checklist for the evidence request, AI-CTRL-038 with TC-L02-016 and TC-L02-017 for evidence packs and auditor access, and AI-CTRL-044 for competence. The auditing firm's own independence rules have no wiki counterpart.
    7.10 AI-assisted audit procedures: approved scope, confidentiality, tool validation, traceable evidence, competent human review, and AI output that does not replace evidence or judgement Gap. The wiki governs AI use by the business, not AI use by the audit team. The acceptable-use standard under AI-CTRL-012 and the oversight measures in AI-CTRL-045 are the nearest material to reuse for an audit function's own policy.
    10 AI attack surface, infrastructure hardening, secure MLOps and LLMOps, AI-specific VAPT, SOC integration, incident response and business continuity, tier-specific cybersecurity requirements AI-CTRL-032, AI-CTRL-030, AI-CTRL-034, AI-CTRL-009, AI-CTRL-035 and AI-CTRL-040, with the Red Team Playbook and the MITRE ATLAS and OWASP crosswalks the section names
    11 Contestability and human decision review: review triggers, the information a reviewer gets, escalation, reviewer independence from the model build, effectiveness monitoring, feedback into model governance AI-CTRL-045 and AI-CTRL-023 from the operator's side, and TC-L03-008 for a person's right to object and ask for human review. The audit tests in 11.3, in particular whether an approval gate can be bypassed through another interface or API, are covered by TC-L06-016. Reviewer independence from the development team is not a wiki control.
    12 Four finding severities with remediation timelines, finding documentation, report structure, assurance opinion, corrective action plans, remediation tracking and closure AI-CTRL-038 with TC-L02-018, and the AI Risk Treatment Plan. The wiki does not set audit severities or opinion wording; take both from the Guidelines, which tie Critical findings to containment and Moderate ones to a 30-day plan and 60-day implementation.
    13 Five maturity levels, from ad hoc to continuously optimised, with indicators for each of the nine domains The Maturity Model and Roadmap. Both run five levels and read similarly, but the Guidelines index theirs to the nine domains, so score against their table when the audience is an Indian auditor.
    15 Alignment with the India AI Governance Guidelines: the seven sutras and the six policy pillars The India AI Regulatory Hub, row IN4 for the MeitY guidelines and row IN14 for these Guidelines

    Where the wiki stops

    The Guidelines are an auditing framework, so part of them is about the audit rather than the AI system: empanelment, engagement letters, auditor competence and independence, working papers, sampling, report structure, assurance opinions and the controlled use of AI in audit work. The wiki produces the evidence an auditor asks for and does not try to govern the auditing firm. Use sections 7, 12 and 13 of the Guidelines for that, with ISACA or IIA methodology.

    On the AI system itself, five requirements have no control behind them yet:

    1. Validation by a function independent of the model developers, covering conceptual soundness and stress and edge-case testing (8.3). The risk is on the register as AI-R39.
    2. Proxy-variable screening of the feature set (8.4).
    3. Counterfactual fairness testing for high-impact decision models (8.4).
    4. Explanation quality tested for fidelity, stability, consistency and reproducibility, and a documented choice of explainability technique with its limitations (8.5).
    5. Cyber-physical safety: sensor integrity, interlocks independent of the model, hardware-in-the-loop testing and enforced physical limits (9.10).

    The first four are in scope for this wiki and are candidates for the next control and test case update. The fifth is a different discipline, closer to functional safety than to AI security, and the wiki does not plan to cover it.
