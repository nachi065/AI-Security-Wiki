# International AI Instruments Crosswalk

> **Purpose:** Map two international AI instruments that are not tied to one country to the wiki's controls and templates: the first international treaty on AI, and the G7 code of conduct for developers of advanced AI.

> **Audience:** Legal and compliance teams, AI governance leads, providers of advanced and general-purpose AI models, vendor evaluation teams.

> **Using the two together:** Use these instruments for the commitments. Use this wiki for the controls, evidence and templates. National laws are in the regional sections, starting with the [EU AI Regulatory Hub](../12_EU_AI_Compliance/01_EU_AI_Regulatory_Hub.md).

## Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (CETS No. 225)

**Status: signed, not in force.** Opened for signature on 5 September 2024 (Council of Europe [information page](https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence); [treaty text](https://rm.coe.int/1680afae3c); [explanatory report](https://rm.coe.int/1680afae67)). Article 30(3) says it enters into force three months after five signatories, including at least three member States of the Council of Europe, have consented to be bound. The European Union ratified it on 15 May 2026 ([Council of Europe news](https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/-/european-union-ratifies-the-council-of-europe-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence)). A secondary tracker reported no other consents as of 8 September 2026. Check the current position at the [Treaty Office](https://www.coe.int/en-GB/web/conventions/full-list2?module=signatures-by-treaty&treatynum=225) before relying on it. The Council of Europe lists 21 signatories, including the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Japan, Israel, Switzerland, Norway, Ukraine and the European Union.

The Convention binds states, not organizations directly. Parties apply it to public authorities and to private actors acting for them, and choose how to apply it to the rest of the private sector. National defence is excluded. It matters to organizations because the laws that implement it will.

## G7 Hiroshima Process International Code of Conduct for Organizations Developing Advanced AI Systems

Published by the G7 on 30 October 2023 ([text](https://www.soumu.go.jp/hiroshimaaiprocess/pdf/document05_en.pdf); [European Commission page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/node/12130)). It is voluntary and addressed to organizations that develop the most advanced AI systems, including foundation and generative models. The OECD runs a voluntary reporting framework built on the Code's 11 actions; it launched in February 2025 and is now in its second version ([OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/transparency/about)). *The actions were read from the Code's text on 15 October 2026.*

The Code overlaps strongly with the EU's GPAI Code of Practice, which providers can use to show compliance with the AI Act's GPAI obligations ([EU T12](../12_EU_AI_Compliance/T12_GPAI_Model_Checklist.md)). When you buy from a developer of advanced models, ask whether it has published a report under the Hiroshima reporting framework (see the [Vendor Evaluation Master Framework](../05_Vendor_Evaluation/11_AI_Security_Vendor_Evaluation_Master_Framework.md)).

## Attribution

The Framework Convention is a Council of Europe treaty. The Hiroshima Process Code of Conduct is a G7 document. This page cites article and action numbers and short titles, and does not reproduce their text beyond the short quotation of Article 30(3).
