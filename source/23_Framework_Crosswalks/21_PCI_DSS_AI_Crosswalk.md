# PCI DSS AI Crosswalk

> **Purpose:** Let a team that runs AI systems in or near a cardholder data environment find the wiki controls and test cases behind each point of the PCI Security Standards Council's AI guidance, and the PCI DSS requirements that guidance applies to AI.

> **Audience:** Payment security and compliance teams, QSAs and ISAs preparing for an assessment, security architects, AI platform owners at merchants and service providers.

> **Using the two together:** Use PCI DSS for the requirements and the PCI SSC supplement for how they apply to AI. Use this wiki for the controls, evidence and test cases that show each point is in place. The supplement is guidance only, and so is this page: neither changes what a PCI DSS assessment requires.

## About the guidance and this page

**Security Considerations for AI Systems** is an information supplement from the PCI Security Standards Council, version 1.0, dated September 2026. It covers two things: securing AI systems that entities in scope of PCI standards deploy, and defending payment environments against attackers who use AI. It does not cover AI that makes payments (agentic commerce), and it does not cover the use of AI in PCI assessments, which PCI SSC addresses in a separate document, *Integrating Artificial Intelligence in PCI Assessments Guidelines*. The supplement states that it does not replace or supersede requirements in any PCI SSC standard, and that requirement references are to the versions current when it was published; it names PCI DSS v4.0.1.

Earlier, on 11 September 2025, PCI SSC published a blog post, *AI Principles: Securing the Use of AI in Payment Environments*, with high-level principles. The supplement is the longer guidance.

The supplement's main ideas, in its own terms:

- **Limits of AI use.** At a minimum an AI system should not have access to cleartext high-impact secrets, generate random data or high-impact secrets, make decisions no human is accountable for, share credentials or act in ways that are not uniquely identifiable, or reach systems it does not need.
- **Least agency.** Give an AI system only the access, data and ability to respond to input that its tasks need.
- **The lethal trifecta.** No AI system should combine access to sensitive data, exposure to untrusted content and external communication. Work that needs all three is split across agents, each with only one or two.
- **Human in the loop or over the loop.** Per-action approval may be replaced by approval of an agent's scope and functions, but a named, suitable person stays accountable, and the supplement lists the questions to answer first. For cleartext cardholder data it advises keeping per-action approval.
- **An AI system is not a person.** It stores rather than remembers, so anything that may not be stored in cleartext should not be given to it in cleartext; and it should be treated as a potential malicious insider, scoped by the network and system constraints it actually runs under, not by its prompt.
- **Scope follows reach.** An AI system that receives or can reach account data is in scope of PCI DSS; one that holds only tokens or encrypted data may be out of scope, but not if it can also reach the detokenisation system or key service.

> **Verify before use.** The supplement's content and the requirement numbers in the first table were read from a copy of the PDF that the wiki maintainers received on 29 October 2026 [Verified against that copy]. Its publication was reported by Help Net Security on 9 October 2026 [Reported]; the copy in the PCI SSC Document Library could not be reached from the environment used to write this page, so check that your copy matches it. Requirement numbers marked [Recalled] are not cited by the supplement and come from this wiki's own reading of PCI DSS v4.0.1; check them against the standard before relying on them. The mapping is this wiki's own judgement and has not been reviewed by PCI SSC.

**Match** is **Direct** when the wiki has a control and at least one test case for the point as it applies to AI, **Partial** when it covers only part, as the notes explain, and **Gap** when the wiki does not cover it.

## Scoping an AI system for PCI DSS

The supplement's examples reduce to a few checks. Use them as a starting point for discussion with your assessor, not as a scoping decision.

1. Does the AI system receive or have access to account data, or have unrestricted connectivity to systems that do? If yes, it is part of the cardholder data environment.
2. Does it hold only tokens or data encrypted with strong cryptography? Then list every system and identity it can reach. If any of them can detokenise or decrypt, or can grant that access (for example an access management console), treat the AI system as having cleartext access ([TC-L10-035](../10_Test_Case_Library/L10-data-layer.md#tc-l10-035)).
3. If it is isolated from those paths, the isolation controls are in scope and must be shown to work, even if the AI system itself is not.
4. Include the data used for training and the data stores the AI system creates, not only the data it reads at run time.
5. Where any part of the AI system is run by an external party and can reach sensitive data, that party is a third-party service provider.

## Where the wiki stops

The supplement has two halves. This wiki is about the first, securing AI systems, and covers most of it. The second half, defending all systems against attackers who use AI, is recorded as register risk [AI-R55](../02_Risk_Management/02B_Enterprise_AI_Risk_Register.md#ai-r55-ai-accelerated-exploitation-and-patch-gap), but its treatment is mostly general security practice: asset inventory, continuous vulnerability scanning and triage, patching speed, phishing-resistant authentication, segmentation, encryption and key management in a KMS or HSM. The wiki does not repeat that practice. Use PCI DSS and general security guidance for it.

The wiki also does not map the PCI PIN, PCI KMO, PCI MPoC or other PCI standards that the supplement mentions, and it does not cover AI used in PCI assessments or for making payments.
