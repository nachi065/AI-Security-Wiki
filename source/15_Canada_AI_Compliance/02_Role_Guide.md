# Canada: Role Guide

> **Verification required.** Claims on this page about Canadian law were compiled on 7 October 2026 from secondary web sources and from memory of the law. No primary legal text was read, and each claim is tagged [Reported], [Recalled], [Conflict] or [Unverified] as explained on the [section home page](index.md). Check the primary text before relying on any of them. This is not legal advice.

Use the [Canada AI Regulatory Hub](01_Canada_AI_Regulatory_Hub.md) for instrument detail, and the templates in this section.

## AI practitioners

For engineers, data scientists and security staff.

- Capture the facts that Quebec requires: the factors behind each automated decision and the human review route ([CA-T2](CA-T2_Quebec_Law_25_ADM_Record.md)).
- For public-sector work, run an impact assessment and set the impact level ([CA-T1](CA-T1_Algorithmic_Impact_Assessment_Worksheet.md)).
- Limit the reuse of data in training beyond its original purpose.

## Product companies

For vendors, SaaS providers and platforms.

- Public-sector bids need AIA-style evidence.
- Quebec customers expect explanation and human review features.
- Financial institutions will ask for OSFI-style model documentation.

## Auditors

For internal, external and assurance auditors.

- Define the criteria by province and sector.
- Test Law 25 notices, explanations and review.
- State that no federal AI law applies.

## Implementors

For programme leads, GRC teams, CISOs and DPOs.

- Create a Canada overlay with federal, Quebec and sector layers.
- Keep a watchlist of bills.
- Align the model inventory with OSFI expectations if they apply to you ([CA-T3](CA-T3_OSFI_E23_Inventory_Addendum.md)).

## Shared evidence list

- AIA records
- Law 25 notices and explanation logs
- Model inventory
- Purpose and consent records
